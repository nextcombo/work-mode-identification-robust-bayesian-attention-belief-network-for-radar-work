# [Work Mode Identification] Robust Bayesian Attention Belief Network for Radar Work Mode Identification [with Python Code]

Understanding and analyzing the role of radar operational modes in electronic support measures systems is crucial. Currently, numerous classifiers, such as those based on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), are available for identifying their operational modes and radiation source types from radar waveform parameters. However, these methods can significantly degrade performance when encountering different types of signal degradations, such as measurement errors, pulse loss, and false pulses. To address this issue, this paper develops a Bayesian Attention Belief Network (BABNet) based on Bayesian Neural Networks, wherein the probability distribution of weights helps enhance the model's robustness to damaged data. Specifically, we employ pre-trained CNNs as priors for Bayesian inference, which not only accelerates convergence but also avoids training processes falling into bad local minima. Additionally, the proposed BABNet incorporates padding operations and attention modules, replacing the difficultly parallelizable RNN, enabling the CNN serving as the backbone network to handle variable-length sequence data. Extensive experiments have demonstrated the recognition capability and robustness of BABNet across various environments.
Introduction to Specific Radiation Source Identification (SEI) involves distinguishing or identifying different radiation sources (such as cognitive radios [1], Wi-Fi devices [2], or Internet of Things devices [3]) by extracting radio frequency fingerprint features from intercepted signals. In military applications, SEI is used to identify specific models of similar radars in modern battlefield electronic intelligence systems. This task requires the development of effective methods to extract unique features from radar signals.
For radar radiation sources, radio frequency (RF) characteristics can generally be divided into inter-pulse and intra-pulse features [6]. These two types of features, along with complex modulation patterns and dense electromagnetic environments, result in highly diverse and unpredictable radiation signals [7]. Specifically, the parameters for the incident radar signal include RF, pulse repetition interval (PRI), pulse width (PW), pulse amplitude, arrival angle, arrival time, and scanning type [8]. The aforementioned parameters can be used to describe and analyze the general state (i.e., operational mode) of the radar radiation source, including search, capture, tracking, imaging, and missile guidance [9]. Therefore, once these parameters are determined, it becomes possible to identify the type, function, and platform of the radiation source, providing intelligence for electronic support measures (ESM) systems to perform threat detection and regional surveillance tasks [10]. Additionally, each pulse of the radar signal may also contain modulation, namely intra-pulse modulation, which includes intentional and unintentional modulations. The former refers to specific changes in amplitude, phase, and frequency over time, such as the SEI method based on intentional modulation modes found in literature [11]. Unintentional modulation features, such as the leading edge of the pulse, instantaneous amplitude [12], and the temporal accumulation of the pulse signal [13], are usually associated with hardware defects. This paper primarily focuses on inter-pulse features in SEI.
Deep learning technology has significantly improved the performance of various recognition tasks. In recent years, supervised classification methods based on convolutional neural networks (CNN) or recurrent neural networks (RNN) have been introduced in SEI [14] and pulse-intermodulation type identification. Since RNNs learn the implicit dependencies among all previous elements in a sequence, they are generally better suited for processing sequential inputs, hence some RNN-based methods have been proposed for handling radar signal stream data. In literature [15], the sequence patterns of continuous pulses are first represented by a series of PRI and PW, followed by training a standard Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) to perform pulse stream pattern classification, denoising, and deinterleaving. Literature [16] assigns a separate GRU for each interpulse feature and introduces an attention mechanism into radar radiation source classification, proving effective in cases with high loss rates and high false pulse rates. Compared to coarse-grained "seq2one" schemes that take a pulse sequence as input to obtain a category label, literature [17] proposes a hierarchical Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, generating a label sequence for each pulse to accurately determine the transition boundaries of each class. Moreover, by forming unique local patterns through CNNs, another alternative approach is provided for extracting relevant semantic information from radar sequence data. Literature [18] constructs a one-dimensional CNN model with 4 convolutional layers, 2 max pooling layers, and 2 fully connected (FC) layers to distinguish different PRI modulation types. Specifically, as proposed in literature [19,20], CNNs and RNNs can be combined to recognize sequence-based radar signals using joint temporal and spatial features.
Deep learning methods (such as the aforementioned) may experience significant performance degradation when encountering different types of non-ideal scenarios, such as measurement errors, pulse loss, and spurious pulses. One of the main reasons is that these methods are prone to unreasonable high confidence extrapolation from training data, thus failing to generalize well to noisy test data [21]. Several solutions have been proposed to address this issue. For example, literature [22] suggests using transfer learning to leverage common knowledge between data with varying signal-to-noise ratios (SNR) to obtain robust features resistant to noise interference. However, this method requires clean and noisy data for training, and if the SNR of the test data exceeds that of the training data, recognition performance may decrease. Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) [23-27] have been applied in fields such as visual analysis and speech recognition [24,25]. They place probabilistic distributions on the weights of neural networks to model uncertainty and predict results for data outside the distribution. This paper focuses on the problem of radar operating mode identification, constructing a Bayesian Attentional Belief Network (BABNet) to learn the temporal dependencies of radar sequence inter-pulse data. Specifically, due to the insufficient representation learning capability of standard Transformer structures in cases where the number of training samples is limited, we simplify the encoder-decoder attention layers in BABNet by adopting the "key-query-value" framework from previous work (e.g., [26,27]) without compromising recognition performance.
Conclusion This paper proposes a novel deep learning approach, Bayesian Attention Belief Network (BABNet), for identifying radar operating modes from inter-pulse parameters. We demonstrate that by modeling the weights of each module as a probability distribution, traditional CNNs can be recast as Bayesian neural networks. The proposed BABNet leverages pre-trained CNNs as priors to learn more robust representations of sequence data. Additionally, the combination of padding operations and attention mechanisms enables CNNs to replace RNNs as backbone networks, effectively and efficiently processing variable-length sequence input data. Experimental results show that the proposed BABNet achieves lightweight architectures with performance superior to state-of-the-art methods based on CNNs, RNNs, or autoencoders. Moreover, the proposed BABNet with carefully designed priors exhibits a flatter one-dimensional loss landscape, indicating its advantages in robustness. Future research may focus on further improving recognition performance under pulse loss conditions by considering frameworks such as few-shot learning with Bayesian inference.
